Event cards, phone mockups, dashboards, QR attendance, analytics, galleries, and multiple CTA routes are category-familiar and make the product feel interchangeable with an event marketplace or club-management SaaS.

## Overall Impression

The first viewport earns attention and demonstrates real product thinking. The single biggest opportunity is to make the rest of the page prove the same product: a trusted loop that turns scattered notices and posters into a verified, personalized feed. Right now the page is an impressive showcase but an ambiguous proposition.

## What's Working

1. **The hero is memorable and specific.** The headline is concise, emotionally legible, and supported by a live inbox preview rather than a static mockup.
2. **The source-aware inbox has strong interaction foundations.** Filters, detail view, back navigation, live result count, focus states, and transparent example-data language make the core behavior understandable.
3. **The poster extraction flow has unusually good product hygiene.** Validation, missing-field states, retry/manual-entry fallback, reduced motion, and human-review framing support the trust promise.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The page contradicts the focused MVP

**Why it matters:** Registrations, tickets, QR attendance, participation records, turnout analytics, and “Register now” imply unsupported operational integrations that PRODUCT.md explicitly rules out. This is a trust and positioning failure.

**Fix:** Remove or demote attendance, ticketing, analytics, and registration claims. Make organizer content about upload → AI extraction → ambiguity review → provenance → approved publication. Keep future capabilities out of the primary story.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P1] The page lacks one dominant narrative

**Why it matters:** It shifts from campus inbox to events marketplace to club dashboard to poster parser to lifestyle gallery. Visitors must reconstruct what UniFlow is.

**Fix:** Structure the page as Discover → Verify → Act. Put source verification directly after the hero and move poster-to-event into the main proof sequence. Reduce or relocate events, dashboard, and gallery content.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill`

### [P1] Trust is stated but not demonstrated

**Why it matters:** “Check the original source” is UniFlow’s differentiator, yet the preview exposes only “Example source” text rather than publisher, URL, document date, publication timestamp, and verification state.

**Fix:** Add a clearly fictional but realistic verification panel and show an ambiguous/missing-field review state. Make source status visible as a semantic state, not supporting copy.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P2] Audience and role boundaries are unclear

**Why it matters:** The page targets students, clubs, organizers, and administrators at similar visual weight while the hero speaks to students. First-time visitors do not know who UniFlow is primarily for.

**Fix:** State the student audience immediately, then provide one compact secondary path for authorized publishers. Avoid giving clubs an equally large product surface unless they are the intended publishing cohort.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P2] Fictional data sits too close to institutional specificity

**Why it matters:** Specific dates, venues, clubs, and “Built for life at NIT Hamirpur” can be mistaken for live or endorsed information.

**Fix:** Use one persistent “Illustrative prototype data” treatment, avoid official-sounding fictional event titles, and pair the NIT Hamirpur reference with “Independent prototype; not an official NIT Hamirpur service.”

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P3] Interaction and section volume dilute conversion

**Why it matters:** Filters, tabs, modals, event cards, organizer tabs, extraction demos, galleries, and multiple CTAs make interactivity a substitute for clarity.

**Fix:** Keep only the Today & Soon inbox and poster-to-event extraction interactive on the landing page. Use one primary CTA and one publisher CTA.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan — first-time student:** May interpret UniFlow as an event calendar rather than a trusted campus information hub; can mistake fictional dates and venues for live NIT Hamirpur updates; cannot immediately see how source verification protects against stale WhatsApp/PDF/Instagram information; sees unsupported registration and participation language.

**Alex — power user:** Sees familiar event cards and filters but no clear personalization, saved items, deadline prioritization, source history, or notification value. The page does not show why UniFlow is faster than checking multiple groups and portals.

**Riya — authorized publisher:** The poster extraction demo is the most relevant workflow but is buried after unrelated student-facing content. The page overemphasizes attendance/analytics and underexplains authorization, human review, provenance, ambiguity, and approved publication.

## Minor Observations

- Pair “Built for life at NIT Hamirpur” with an explicit independent/non-endorsed qualifier.
- The fixed “Week of 19 October 2026” and many dated examples make the landing page feel time-bound.
- Naming shifts between campus inbox, events, tickets, organizer tools, and poster extraction.
- The photo gallery is emotionally attractive but currently decorative rather than proof-bearing.
- The detector flags undocumented compact text sizes; either align them to the documented type ramp or document the intentional metadata scale.
- Increase muted text contrast and avoid 10.4px functional labels.
- Review `-0.06em` display tracking, 0px vertical padding controls, long body lines, and nested-card treatments.
- Add a favicon to remove the current 404.

## Questions to Consider

1. If UniFlow disappeared tomorrow, would students say they lost an event calendar or a trusted way to know what matters next?
2. Why does the page spend more visual energy on attendance analytics than on proving source authenticity?
3. Is NIT Hamirpur the launch context, or is it being used as borrowed institutional credibility?
4. Which single action should a visitor take after 30 seconds, and does every major section support it?
5. Are the event-management demos intentional roadmap communication, or accidental unsupported product claims?
6. Where does verification visibly happen in the first two scrolls?
